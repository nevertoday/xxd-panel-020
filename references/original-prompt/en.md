Please make every photo I upload into its own standalone high-end design poster; no multi-image collages, each photo output separately. The overall composition uses a 3:4 vertical format, with the upper and lower zones strictly 1:1 in height, each taking up 50% of the frame.

The upper half keeps the original photo, preserving the subject's structure, spatial relationships, real materials, natural light and shadow, and original color atmosphere, applying only a slight high-end photographic color grade so that it carries the texture of art magazines, independent publications, and exhibition photography. To fit the frame, the environmental background may be extended naturally, but the subject must not be stretched, distorted, or altered.

Against a clean ivory background, the lower half extracts the most recognizable subject, outline, pose, and narrative relationships from the photo and transforms them into a minimalist abstract visual according to the logic of “deconstruct—select—distill—reconstruct.” Focus on retaining the most distinctive relationships of volume and proportion, axes, curves, interweaving, layering, occlusion, negative space, and asymmetry, reorganizing them with a small number of geometric forms. Do not reproduce every detail, but the correspondence with the original image above must be immediately perceptible. The composition emphasizes visual focus, differences in scale, primary and secondary hierarchy, positive and negative shapes, and the rhythm of negative space. Avoid iconization, evenly distributed arrangements, and fragmented accumulation.

Extract all colors from the photo above and simplify them to 1 main color, 1 dark structural color, and 1 light/neutral color, adding at most a small amount of a key accent color. Prioritize the most distinctive and spirited colors from the original image, and establish depth through proportional areas, light-dark relationships, and local contrast. Keep the palette clean, restrained, and vital; avoid making the whole image gray and using colors evenly.

The main title conveys mood, while the small text provides order and detail, making the text resemble editorial annotations in an art book, architecture journal, or exhibition catalogue rather than an ordinary title.

The text must share the composition with the abstract forms. One text group may be extremely small and another slightly larger; use contrasts in type size, letter spacing, direction, and position to establish a reading path. Throughout, combine restrained serif typefaces with slender editorial typefaces, letting the abstract forms carry visual memory and the text provide structure, rhythm, and a high-end quality.

The overall work presents a visual character of real photography × abstract memory × editorial typography: modern, quiet, restrained, refined, artistic, and collectible. Avoid complex collage, cheap decoration, a commercial-template feel, and meaningless abstraction.
